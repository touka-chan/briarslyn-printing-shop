 TITLE PROPOSAL 1PROPOSED TITLE: An Integrated Mobile Point-of-Sale, IoT-Based Inventory Monitoring, and
Order-Workflow System for Custom Printing Businesses
I. PROJECT OVERVIEW AND RATIONALE
[Introduce the client, existing workflow, verified problem, affected users, and why an integrated solution is
needed. Support major claims with interview evidence or credible references.]
Brialyns Art Sign is a custom print shop owned by Jena Bersamina. The business provides tarpaulins,
invitations, giveaways, screen prints, and signs. Currently, the owner and staff process customer orders,
handle pricing discussions, and track inventory manually using paper logbooks, direct social media
messages, and occasional Excel sheets.
Because there is no centralized tracking system, staff face difficulties knowing whether sufficient materials
are in stock for incoming jobs and determining which orders to process first when handling different target
completion dates. This manual process leads to excessive time spent answering customer inquiries,
confused inventory management, and delayed order completions. An integrated solution combining a
role-based mobile app for staff and a web dashboard for the owner is needed to digitize order creation,
automate priority queues, track material stock levels, and provide visibility over shop operations.
To further address material shortages before they cause delays, the system will integrate an ESP32-based
RFID check-out station for key printing materials to capture real-time usage events. Each material variant
(e.g., a specific tarpaulin size, ink color, or paper type) is assigned one RFID tag, which staff tap whenever a
unit of that material is taken from storage. This usage data feeds a predictive analytics layer that calculates
dynamic reorder points and forecasts material demand, while a separate model estimates realistic order
completion dates based on the shop's live workload. This moves the business from reactive stock-checking
to proactive planning.
II. STATEMENT OF THE PROBLEM
[State the main operational problem as a clear question or problem statement. Identify two to four specific
process or information gaps.]
Inefficient Order and Schedule Tracking- Reliance on paper logbooks and handwritten notes makes it
difficult to track order status and completion target dates.
Lack of Order Prioritization- Staff have no automated system to identify which orders are overdue, urgent,
or upcoming, causing confusion in production.
Manual Inventory Monitoring- Printing supplies (blank shirts, tarpaulin, mugs, paper) are not tracked
automatically, leading to sudden material shortages during production.
No Real-Time Physical Stock Visibility- Even with digital order tracking, material counts are only as
accurate as the last manual check, so the owner still discovers shortages late instead of getting advance
warning based on actual usage.
No Realistic Completion Estimates- The shop has no way to predict how long an order will actually take
based on current backlog and job complexity, leading to missed target dates and inconsistent customer
expectations.

III. PROJECT OBJECTIVES
General objective
[One outcome-focused objective for the complete integrated system.]
To design and develop a client order tracking, production scheduling, inventory management, and predictive
analytics system that helps a small printing business organize customer orders, prioritize production based
on target dates, monitor the availability of printing materials in real time, and forecast material and
completion needs.
Specific objectives
1. To develop a role-based mobile application that enables POS/Cashier staff to create orders, upload
design layouts, record payments, and set target pick-up dates.
2. To provide a Production Staff mobile interface with a production queue that automatically prioritizes
orders according to target dates.
3. To implement real-time order status tracking from Pending to In Production, Ready for Pickup, and
Completed.
4. To track printing material inventory and display low-stock or insufficient-stock indicators for upcoming
jobs.
5. To build a web-based Admin/Owner system featuring dashboards, user management, production
schedules, and inventory monitoring.
6. To provide the Admin/Owner with production and inventory analytics, including order volume trends,
on-time versus overdue completion rates, and material usage trends, along with a basic material restock
forecast based on recent consumption trends.
7. To integrate an ESP32-based RFID check-out station, with one RFID tag assigned per material variant
(e.g., tarpaulin size, ink color, paper type), to log real-time material usage events.
8. To implement a predictive inventory forecasting model that calculates dynamic Reorder Points (ROP) and
forecasts material demand using time-series analysis (e.g., Holt-Winters or Exponential Smoothing) based
on real-time RFID check-out data.
9. To develop a dynamic ETA feature that predicts realistic order completion dates based on live shop
backlog, job complexity, and current operational capacity.

TITLE PROPOSAL 1 - INTEGRATION DESIGN
VII. PROPOSED INTEGRATED WORKFLOW
[Write or draw the end-to-end journey. Identify who or what creates an event, what data moves, which
component processes it, and what verified outcome follows.]
The workflow begins when a customer provides their order details, contact information, design
requirements, and requested completion or pick-up date. The POS/Cashier, using the mobile application,
creates the event by encoding this information into the system. This includes the customer's details, the
items and quantity, the uploaded design layout, the recorded payment, and the target completion or pick-up
date.
Once the order is saved, the system checks the required printing materials against current inventory. If
sufficient stock is available, the order is confirmed as successfully created. If not, the system displays an
insufficient stock indicator, prompting the Admin/Owner to restock. This is the first verified outcome of the
flow, and it directly supports Functional Requirement 6, which covers monitoring inventory and flagging low
or insufficient stock.
The confirmed order data is stored in the Centralized Database, which the Production Staff accesses
through the mobile app's Production Queue. Per Functional Requirement 3, the system automatically
prioritizes orders based on their target dates, identifying which are overdue, urgent, or upcoming, so
Production Staff can determine which orders to process first. As work progresses, the Production Staff
updates the order status through the defined flow of Pending, then In Production, then Ready for Pickup,
then Completed, as described in Functional Requirement 5. Each status update is saved and synced back
to the database, serving as the verified outcome that confirms production progress.
In parallel, the Admin/Owner, through the web-based system, continuously monitors the same Centralized
Database, viewing orders, production schedules, inventory, users, and reports through the dashboard, as
described in Functional Requirement 7. This allows the Admin/Owner to oversee overall operations and
respond to alerts such as low-stock indicators.
In parallel with the order and inventory workflow, staff tap the RFID tag of a material variant (e.g., Tarpaulin -
Medium, Ink - Black) at the ESP32-based check-out station whenever a unit of that material is taken from
storage. Each tap is logged as a check-out event and transmitted to the backend in real time, which deducts
one unit from that variant's tracked stock count. The backend feeds this accumulated check-out history into
a time-series forecasting model that calculates a dynamic reorder point per material variant, factoring in
recent consumption trends. When stock approaches this calculated threshold, the system generates a
reorder alert on the Admin/Owner dashboard, ahead of an actual stockout rather than after it.
Separately, each time an order is created or its status changes, the backend recalculates that order's
estimated completion date (ETA) using the current production backlog, the complexity of the job, and
available staff capacity. This ETA is shown to Production Staff and the Admin/Owner, and updates
automatically as the queue shifts.
Once an order reaches Completed status, the responsibility shifts back to the business staff to physically
hand over the finished item to the customer, since actual printing, production, and customer pick-up remain
manual staff responsibilities, as stated in the system's Limitations.
